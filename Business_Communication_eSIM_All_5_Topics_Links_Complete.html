Learning outcomes

By the end of this learning session, you should be able to explain the business communication process, identify effective communication skills, recognise common barriers, and select appropriate digital communication tools for workplace situations.

1. Overview of Business Communication

Business communication is the exchange of information inside or outside an organisation to achieve work-related goals. It supports coordination, decision-making, relationships and performance.

View SlidesWatch Video

2. Communication Process

The sender develops an idea, encodes a message, chooses a channel and sends it. The receiver interprets it and may provide feedback. Noise or interference can affect any stage.

View SlidesWatch Video

3. Effective Communication Skills

Use clear language, active listening, suitable tone, empathy, concise messages and feedback. Adapt your wording and channel to the audience and purpose.

View SlidesWatch Video

4. Barriers to Communication

Common barriers include unclear wording, jargon, language differences, assumptions, emotional reactions, poor listening, cultural differences and technical problems. Check understanding and invite questions.

View SlidesWatch Video

5. Digital Communication Tools

Email suits formal, traceable messages; instant messaging suits brief coordination; video meetings support discussion; shared documents support collaboration. Consider urgency, confidentiality, audience and record-keeping.

View SlidesWatch Video

Quick application: choose the channel

Scenario: You need to discuss a sensitive performance issue with a colleague and allow time for questions.

Suggested approach: Arrange a private face-to-face or video conversation. A short message can arrange the meeting, but avoid discussing sensitive details in a public group chat. Follow up in writing only when appropriate.

Teaching prompt: What could go wrong if the same issue were sent to a large group chat? Consider privacy, tone and interpretation.

Reflection

Before finishing, think of one workplace message you have recently sent or received. Was the purpose clear? Was the channel appropriate? What feedback would help confirm understanding?

Key takeaway: Effective business communication is purposeful, audience-centred, clear and supported by feedback.
